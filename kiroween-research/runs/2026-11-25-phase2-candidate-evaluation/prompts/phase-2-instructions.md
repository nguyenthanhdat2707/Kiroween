## A. Candidate Selection Rubric and International Market Orientation (verbatim)

## Candidate Selection Rubric

Score each candidate from 0–5 against the following weighted criteria:

- Real Problem & User Demand: 20%
- International Relevance: 15%
- Innovation & Differentiation: 15%
- Resurrection Theme Fit: 15%
- Technical & AI Contribution: 10%
- Implementation Feasibility: 10%
- Demo & Storytelling Potential: 10%
- Long-term Sustainability: 5%

Distinguish this candidate-selection rubric from the existing reference-quality rubric and the official Kiroween judging criteria.

Before scoring, establish shared scoring anchors. Agents may challenge the rubric, but must not independently change its weights during evaluation.

Evaluate candidates independently before debate. After debate, record revised scores, changed judgments, counterarguments, and remaining disagreements.

Perform sensitivity analysis to determine whether reasonable changes in criterion weights would change the Top 3.

## International Market Orientation

Prioritize problems that are internationally relevant, particularly those supported by evidence from multiple geographic regions.

- Investigate North American, European, and Asian markets without assuming any region is inherently more important.
- Identify concrete target users rather than claiming everyone is a potential user.
- Evaluate cross-cultural applicability and barriers to international adoption.
- Consider whether the problem can be communicated clearly to an international judging panel.
- Do not penalize cultural or heritage projects merely because they originate from a specific country.
- Explore whether a local cultural problem represents a broader global problem.

For Task B, explicitly investigate digital heritage, endangered online communities, disappearing cultural knowledge, platform continuity, and the preservation of digital civilization.

Do not assume commercial viability or user demand. Validate both.

The final Top 3 must be evidence-driven recommendations, not forced consensus or predictions of judges' preferences.
