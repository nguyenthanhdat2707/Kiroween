# Next Research Cycle — Digital Knowledge Continuity

## 1. Human Direction

After reviewing the Task B decision brief, I am particularly interested in the following opportunity spaces:

- S2 — Maintaining academic and research applications after their funding ends.
- T2 — Helping small SaaS platforms and online communities transfer their valuable assets before shutdown.
- T3 — Preserving disappearing academic journals.
- T4 — Preserving and transferring digital heritage archives.

These represent my personal research interests, not validated conclusions.

I want to investigate whether these opportunities address a broader problem: valuable digital knowledge, functionality, and cultural assets disappearing because the organizations responsible for maintaining them no longer have sufficient resources.

Do not assume that these four opportunities should become one product. Investigate them independently before exploring shared mechanisms.

## 3. Phase 3 — Focused Deep Research

Investigate all four shortlisted opportunity spaces.

For each opportunity, establish:

- The actual problem and its root causes.
- Affected stakeholders and target users.
- Documented real-world cases across multiple countries.
- Evidence of demand and consequences of inaction.
- Existing solutions and their limitations.
- Legal, financial, technical, and organizational barriers.
- Who would pay for or maintain a solution.
- Whether AI can provide meaningful advantages.
- Whether the opportunity genuinely fits the Kiroween Resurrection category.

Prioritize Business Analysis before solution design.

Investigate whether preservation alone is sufficient or whether restoring lost functionality creates additional value.

T3 and T4 previously failed the theme-fit gate. Investigate whether a legitimate resurrection opportunity exists within these spaces without artificially redefining the competition theme.

## 4. Phase 4 — Multi-Agent Cross-review

Allow agents to examine each other's completed research.

Require them to:

- Challenge assumptions and unsupported claims.
- Identify overlooked existing solutions.
- Present the strongest counterarguments.
- Examine whether the four opportunities share an underlying problem.
- Challenge proposals to combine them.
- Identify where preservation is unnecessary or unsustainable.
- Distinguish technical feasibility from actual user demand.

Record disagreements and changes in judgment.

Do not force consensus.

## 5. Phase 5 — Opportunity and Solution Exploration

For opportunities that survive cross-review:

- Generate multiple possible solution approaches.
- Compare AI-enabled and conventional engineering approaches.
- Explore infrastructure, software preservation, migration, archival, and continuity mechanisms where relevant.
- Investigate whether shared technical capabilities could support multiple use cases.
- Identify a narrowly scoped initial target user.
- Define measurable value propositions.
- Identify the smallest useful MVP and its limitations.

Do not build a universal digital preservation platform simply because the opportunities appear related.
